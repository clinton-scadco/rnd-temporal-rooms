One ore line. Three ways to power it.

Each machine does the same job. Compare the prime movers, drive layout and supporting structure, then open its Plan or Form to inspect and change the actual design.

Water and timber

Build around the river

River → water wheels → belts → crushers

Large wooden wheels and braced timber supports. Power is shared along the plant; the crushers sit on cast-iron beds.

Try replacing a belt with a shaft. Crusher vibration reaches the timber wheel and stops it; the belt isolates that vibration.

Steam and cast iron

Make power, then shed heat

Boilers → piston engines → drives → crushers

Cast beds, cylinders, piston rods and spoked flywheels. Boilers and cooling create a different industrial layout.

Disconnect cooling and watch temperature and duty. The steam engine can overheat even while fuel and steam are available.

Electric and steel

Put a motor at each machine

Grid → local motors → crushers

Compact drives and fabricated steel supports. Each crusher can be powered locally; there is no central wheel or boiler house.

Disconnect the grid. Local motors stop with their supply, and electricity consumption remains part of the operating cost.
